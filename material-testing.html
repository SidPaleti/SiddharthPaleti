Engineering Mechanics

Material Testing

Identified two unknown aluminum alloys from tensile tests, with both matches within 5% of reference values.

Role
Specimen prep, testing and analysis
Course
EMCH 213 Honors project, Learning Factory
Equipment
Tensile tester, extensometer, mills, bandsaw
Measured
Yield, tensile strength, modulus

Problem

The objective of this lab was to identify unknown material samples by analyzing their mechanical properties. Accurate identification required standardized sample preparation, reliable testing, and precise data analysis to minimize experimental errors and ensure meaningful conclusions.

Method

To prepare the specimens, I used the Learning Factory’s metal shop mills and wood shop bandsaw to create standardized bone-shaped samples designed to fail in the gauge section rather than at the grips. Each sample was tested using a tensile tester, with an extensometer attached to record elongation and ensure precise stress-strain data. Stress was calculated as the applied force divided by the cross-sectional area, while strain was determined from the change in length over the initial length. Stress-strain curves were then generated, and key mechanical properties—including yield strength, tensile strength, and modulus of elasticity—were compared against reference values to identify the materials.

Solution

For Sample 1, the stress-strain analysis produced a yield strength of 35.7 ksi, tensile strength of 37.5 ksi, and modulus of elasticity of 10 × 10^6 psi, which closely matched aluminum 6062-T6 with less than 5% error. Sample 2 showed a yield strength of 37.9 ksi, tensile strength of 41 ksi, and modulus of elasticity of 10 × 10^6 psi, aligning with aluminum 6151-T6, again within 5% error. Sample 3 produced unreliable results due to grip slippage, highlighting the importance of improved gripping techniques for accurate data collection. Overall, the project demonstrated my ability to extract meaningful insights from experimental data, accurately identify materials, and critically evaluate the reliability of testing methods.
