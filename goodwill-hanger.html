Product design

Goodwill Hanger

A hanger with clips that open and close at the press of a button, designed with a team to make hanging clothes faster for Goodwill employees.

Client
Goodwill
Role
Mechanism design, testing and iteration
Tools
SolidWorks, rapid prototyping
Team
Divided research on materials and mechanism

Problem

Goodwill employees face inefficiencies in the clothing hanging process, and the organization sought a more sustainable product to support daily operations. The challenge was to design a hanger that improved ease of use while addressing sustainability and durability concerns.

Method

My team began by researching the problem, identifying stakeholders, and reviewing existing solutions. Through brainstorming and prototyping, we developed a hanger with clips that open and close at the press of a button. I used SolidWorks to design and refine the mechanism, ensuring reliable operation and durability. The team divided responsibilities: I focused on testing and improving the mechanism, while others investigated sustainable and optimal materials. Prototypes were built and evaluated through multiple iterations, combining individual findings into a final integrated design. Along the way, I also helped motivate teammates and facilitate collaboration when progress slowed.

Solution

The final prototype streamlined the clothing hanging process by enabling employees to secure garments with a simple button press, eliminating the inefficiencies of traditional hangers. The design incorporated sustainable materials, interlocking mechanisms, and durability-focused testing, making it both functional and environmentally conscious. The project enhanced my technical design and problem-solving skills while highlighting the importance of teamwork and iterative prototyping in creating practical, user-centered solutions.
